**Oregon's deep tidepooling water belongs to winter evenings now. Checked October 5 for the rest of 2026: the deepest remaining low in a daylight-overlapping window at each NOAA station — −2.21 ft at Port Orford (9431647), −2.05 ft at Charleston (9432780), −1.91 ft at Newport (9435380), −1.35 ft at Garibaldi (9437540) — arrives after sunset. The deepest low still in daylight is Port Orford's −1.63 ft on December 22 at 4:39 PM, eight minutes before sunset.**

The August 11–14 dawn run — the year's last — came and went as computed; its numbers are kept below as history. What's left of 2026 is an evening season: windows that open in afternoon light and run past dark, deeper the farther south you stand. Here is what remains, what the station math actually covers, and the rules that apply once your boots hit wet sand.

## Oregon's four tidepool stations

"Best tide pools in Oregon" lists tend to name the same shores — Haystack Rock, Yaquina Head, Cape Arago — as if one tide table covers 300 miles of coast. It doesn't. The classic sites cluster around four NOAA reference stations, and what remains of 2026 looks different at each:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Port Orford (Battle Rock, Rocky Point) | Port Orford | 9431647 | −2.21 ft | Dec 24, 6:14 PM (sunset 4:48 PM) | −1.63 ft, Dec 22, 4:39 PM |
| Charleston (Sunset Bay, Cape Arago) | Charleston | 9432780 | −2.05 ft | Dec 24, 6:30 PM (sunset 4:46 PM) | −0.78 ft, Dec 21, 4:05 PM |
| Newport (Yaquina Head, Otter Rock) | Newport (South Beach) | 9435380 | −1.91 ft | Dec 23, 5:53 PM (sunset 4:39 PM) | −0.69 ft, Dec 21, 4:15 PM |
| Cannon Beach (Haystack Rock) | Garibaldi (Tillamook Bay) | 9437540 | −1.35 ft | Dec 22, 5:26 PM (sunset 4:35 PM) | none below 0 ft remain |

*Computed 2026-10-05 from NOAA station predictions, covering October 5–December 31, 2026, station-local dates. "Qualifying" means the modeled below-+1.0-ft window overlaps daylight by at least 30 minutes; as the middle column shows, every one of these deepest lows itself arrives after sunset, so you would work the falling tide in the last of the light, not stand at the low. The right-hand column is the deepest remaining minus-tide low that falls between sunrise and sunset. Access notes per BLM, Oregon State Parks, and the Haystack Rock Awareness Program, re-verified 2026-10-05. Haystack Rock sits about 35 minutes north of its reference station, so treat Cannon Beach clock times as close rather than exact.*

Read down the two right-hand columns and the shape of the season is plain: the deep water is real — Christmas week at Port Orford runs deeper than the August dawn run did there (−2.21 ft against −1.74), though not as deep as mid-July's −2.46 ft — but it arrives in the dark, and the tides you can actually stand in at the bottom are modest, southern, and mostly on weekends. Counting remaining minus-tide lows that themselves happen in daylight: Port Orford has eight (six on weekends, including Sunday, October 25 at −0.51 ft, 6:11 PM, eight minutes before sunset), Charleston four, Newport two (Sunday, November 22 at −0.54 ft, 4:34 PM, and Monday, December 21 at −0.69 ft, 4:15 PM), and Garibaldi none.

On August 12 the low swept the coast south to north in 45 minutes: Port Orford at 6:19 AM, Charleston at 6:35, Newport at 6:46, Garibaldi at 7:04. In summer that lag worked in the north's favor — the later the low, the more of its window fell after sunrise, which is why Garibaldi's 7:04 AM low caught 197 daylight minutes to Port Orford's 149. ## The coast splits at Newport

At every Washington station we compute, 2026's deepest daylight low came in July, in morning light. In California the deepest lows of the year's second half land on Christmas Eve — in actual daylight at La Jolla and San Diego, after sunset at Monterey and Pillar Point. Oregon is the seam between those two regimes, and in 2026 the seam runs through Newport. Here is the deepest low of the king-tide season (late October 2026 through March 2027) in a qualifying daylight-overlap window at each station:

| Station | Deepest king-season qualifying low | When it lands (vs sunset) | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Port Orford (9431647) | −2.21 ft | Dec 24, 6:14 PM (sunset 4:48 PM) | 65 |
| Charleston (9432780) | −2.05 ft | Dec 24, 6:30 PM (sunset 4:46 PM) | 59 |
| Newport (9435380) | −1.91 ft | Dec 23, 5:53 PM (sunset 4:39 PM) | 61 |
| Garibaldi (9437540) | −1.66 ft | Jan 21, 5:59 PM (sunset 5:06 PM) | 57 |

*Computed 2026-10-05 from NOAA station predictions. Every low in this table arrives after sunset. The [2026-2027 king tide calendar](/guides/king-tides-2026-2027-dates/) covers the high-water end of the same alignments.*

The depth column is a staircase: winter's deep water fades as you go north. At Port Orford, Christmas Eve beats the August run by nearly half a foot (−2.21 vs −1.74). At Charleston the winter margin is a third of a foot. At Newport it vanishes — August 12's −1.913 ft against December 23's −1.910 ft, a difference of 0.003 ft, about a millimeter of water — and August's low came in full morning daylight while December's comes 74 minutes after sunset. At Garibaldi the staircase inverts outright: its best king-season low, −1.66 ft on January 21, 2027, never catches its August 12 dawn low of −1.77 ft. South of Newport, winter digs deeper than the August run; north of it, winter never catches August 12. Neither season beats July: the July 13–16 run went deeper still at every station — down to −2.52 ft at Newport on July 15 — so this staircase ranks winter against August, not against the whole year.

The honest catch, now in every row: these lows land after dark. December 23 at Port Orford is the shape of the thing — a −2.07 ft low at 5:26 PM, but the window opens at 2:55 PM with 113 daylight minutes, so the light ends at the 4:48 PM sunset and you work the falling tide, not the low. The cleanest exception comes January 21, 2027 at Port Orford: −1.89 ft at 5:12 PM, four minutes *before* the 5:16 PM sunset, 152 daylight minutes, score 76 — the deepest king-season low at any of the four stations that itself lands in daylight (the only other one below −1.6 ft is December 22's −1.63 ft, also Port Orford), and the highest-scoring window of Oregon's whole king season.

## Which Oregon shore should you pick?

- **Port Orford.** The far south: [Battle Rock Beach, Rocky Point, and Agate Beach](/guides/port-orford-tide-pools-2026/), and the deepest water in Oregon this king season (−2.21 ft on Christmas Eve, after dark) plus the state's best remaining daylight minus tides — December 20–22 is a three-day run of lows in daylight, deepening from −0.05 ft to −1.63 ft as Christmas week opens. The rocks south of Battle Rock run to the Redfish Rocks Marine Reserve boundary — look-only ground since 2012.

- **Charleston.** [Sunset Bay and Cape Arago](/guides/sunset-bay-cape-arago-tide-pools-2026/), open for day use year round with a parking permit. Oregon State Parks describes the South Cove tidepools at Cape Arago as "superior tidepools" and asks that you enjoy them "with your eyes only." The south coast's winter depth without the south coast's drive; its four remaining daylight minus lows are all late-afternoon events, three of them on weekends.

- **Newport.** Two shores, one station, and in winter the [Yaquina Head](/guides/yaquina-head-otter-rock-tide-pools-2026/) gate problem flips ends: the BLM's current posted hours are 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM Monday–Thursday and 8:00 AM to 7:00 PM Friday–Sunday ($7 per vehicle for a 3-day pass; closed Thanksgiving and Christmas), so the deep December 23 low at 5:53 PM sits seven minutes before a Wednesday close — with sunset already 74 minutes gone. The two remaining daylight minus lows (Nov 22, Dec 21) both fit inside posted hours. Open-shore Otter Rock — Oregon's smallest marine reserve at 1.2 square miles, "no take of animals or seaweeds" — has no gate at all. The seabird-nesting access caveat that shadowed Cobble Beach's summer (May through September, per the BLM) is past for the year.

- **Cannon Beach / Haystack Rock.** The open-beach option: no gate, no fee — and in 2026, nothing left to stand at the bottom of in daylight: Garibaldi's remaining minus lows all arrive after sunset. It is an Oregon Marine Garden — "the harvesting of invertebrates and marine aquatic vegetation is strictly prohibited" per the Haystack Rock Awareness Program — with the intertidal only fully open on a real minus tide. ## How to work a window

The "arrive by" time on the station pages is computed as the predicted low minus 60 minutes — a planning offset, not a measure of walking time, route access, or remaining light. In the evening season the number that matters as much is sunset: check it against the low time before you commit to a date, because at most of the dates above the water keeps falling after the light is gone. Wear closed-toe shoes with grip, and give the wildlife its distance; the BLM's rule at Yaquina Head is "stay well away from wildlife and leave rocks, plants, and shells in place," and it travels well. Oregon State Parks' standing advice for this coast: "keep your eyes on the ocean and stay aware of changing tides, sneaker waves, and drift logs."
