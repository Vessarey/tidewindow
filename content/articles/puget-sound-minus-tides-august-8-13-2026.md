**The last Exceptional tidepooling run of 2026 in Puget Sound arrives August 8–13. Sunday, August 9 is the headline day: Port Townsend (NOAA station 9444900) drops to −2.05 ft at 7:38 AM — a perfect-100 window — while Seattle (9447130) reaches −1.91 ft at 8:29 AM. The deepest water waits for Tuesday, August 11: −2.63 ft at 10:19 AM in Seattle, the lowest daylight tide left this year.**

July's famous run put the year's absolute deepest water on a Monday and a Tuesday, which is a fine arrangement for retirees and remote workers and a cruel one for everyone else. August returns the favor. This run opens on a weekend, builds through midweek, and hands the Sound its last 90-plus mornings of the year before the deep lows start sliding toward winter darkness.

## For once, the weekend is the point

Our [scores](/methodology/) add up four things: how deep the low goes (up to 50 points), how much of the walkable window falls in daylight (up to 30), a 10-point bonus for a Saturday, Sunday, or holiday, and up to 10 for seasonal comfort. A weekday can therefore top out at 90 — and Monday through Wednesday of this run all hit exactly that ceiling. Sunday, August 9 is shallower than Tuesday, but it lands on a day most people can actually use, and at Port Townsend its 292 daylight minutes and −2.05 ft low round to the year's only remaining flat 100.

Read that as the scoring being honest about two different questions. Deepest water: Tuesday, by 0.72 ft over Sunday at Seattle and 0.37 ft at Port Townsend. Best morning you probably have free: Sunday.

The usual run shape applies: each low lands roughly 50 minutes later than the day before, so the week starts as an early-riser's tide and ends as a late-morning stroll. On the big Sunday, plan backwards from the low — arrive by 6:38 AM at Port Townsend or 7:29 AM at Seattle to catch the last hour of falling water. Seattle's walkable window that morning runs 6:20 AM to 10:45 AM; Port Townsend's opens even earlier, 5:00 AM to 10:50 AM.

## The outer coast: La Push gets its last two, without Rialto

Out on the Olympic Peninsula's ocean side, La Push (NOAA 9442396) posts its own final Exceptional mornings of 2026: Wednesday, August 12 at −2.31 ft (6:58 AM low, arrive by 5:58 AM) and Thursday, August 13 at −2.14 ft (7:39 AM). Tuesday the 11th is nearly as good at −2.18 ft, 6:13 AM, and Friday the 14th still scores 89 at −1.68 ft, 8:17 AM. Deeper lows do exist on La Push's December calendar, but they land in Fair-scoring near-dark windows — these August mornings are the last of the genuinely good ones.

One access fact matters more than any number here. The National Park Service's Olympic conditions page states: "Mora Road is closed to all traffic for construction from July 8–Oct. 15, 2026," which means Rialto Beach and Hole-in-the-Wall are off the table for this entire run. The place to be is **Second Beach**, reached by a 0.7-mile forest trail off La Push Road — our [La Push guide](/guides/la-push-second-beach-tide-pools-2026/) leads with the closure and covers the trail, the sea stacks, and the rock that a deep low exposes.

## Elsewhere on the coast, briefly

Oregon's four stations peak on Wednesday, August 12, all before breakfast: Newport −1.91 ft at 6:46 AM (score 88), Garibaldi −1.77 ft at 7:04 AM (85), Charleston −1.72 ft at 6:35 AM (81), Port Orford −1.74 ft at 6:19 AM (80). It is the last deep dawn run of the Oregon summer — the [Oregon hub](/guides/best-tide-pools-oregon-2026/) has the full story and all four station guides.

California sits this one out. The same lows arrive there before sunrise — Pillar Point's −1.13 ft on August 12 comes at 5:12 AM, with most of its window in the dark — and the scores stay in the Fair 50s. California's turn comes in winter, when the calendar flips and the deep lows land in daylight afternoons; the [king tides page](/guides/king-tides-2026-2027-dates/) has those dates.

And on the other coast entirely: Acadia's best remaining window of the summer lands the same week, Saturday, August 15 at −0.80 ft, 7:16 AM. The [Acadia guide](/guides/acadia-tide-pools-bar-island-ship-harbor/) covers that run day by day.

## You're on your own this time — pleasantly

July's big lows came with company: the Seattle Aquarium's Beach Naturalist volunteers staffed West Seattle and Golden Gardens beaches through the summer's deepest windows. That season is over — the Aquarium's published 2026 schedule ends with its July 15 session, and no August dates are listed. So this run is self-guided. The species lists on our station pages (recent iNaturalist observations within 5 km of each station) are a reasonable pocket companion — and right now they are, frankly, a sea-slug festival: at [Seattle](/beaches/wa/seattle-wa/), the Monterey Dorid (13 recent observations) and Horned Nudibranch (12) lead the list; at [Port Townsend](/beaches/wa/port-townsend-wa/), the Gumboot Chiton — the world's largest chiton — tops it with 12.

## After this run

Mark it honestly: August 13 closes the Exceptional column for 2026 in the Sound. The best that remains is Monday, September 7 — Labor Day — with −1.11 ft at 7:20 AM in Port Townsend (score 80, Great) and −0.93 ft at 8:12 AM in Seattle (77), and from October the deep water moves to late afternoons and darkness. If a [minus tide](/guides/what-is-a-minus-tide/) is on your summer list, this is the week that decides whether it happens this year.
