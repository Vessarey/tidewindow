## Bar Island: the crossing rule is not our tide-window formula

The [National Park Service's Bar Island Trail page](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-bar-island-trail.htm), checked September 9, describes exposure “For 1.5 hours before and after low tide”. It warns that after missing the crossing, “it is nine hours until the bar emerges again to safely walk across.”

That official guidance and Tidewindow's below-+1.0-ft interval measure different things. Similar clock times do not establish that the gravel bar is exposed. The scores and intervals here do not confirm crossing conditions, reserve time on the island, or guarantee a return route. The linked NPS page contains the full warning and its local tide-chart link.

NPS lists the Bar Island hike at 1.9 miles round trip, including the gravel bar and island trail, starting from Bridge Street in downtown Bar Harbor. Its current access information also says downtown parking is paid from May 15 through October 31 and that parking is not allowed on the bar or Bridge Street. These details are separate from a low-tide prediction.

## Ship Harbor and Wonderland: different places, a regional reference

Tidewindow uses Bar Harbor as its reference station for all three listed Acadia destinations. That does not mean one exact tide time governs every shoreline: the [NPS tidepooling page](https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/tidepooling.htm) provides separate chart links for Bar Island and for Wonderland or Ship Harbor.

- [Ship Harbor](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-ship-harbor-trail.htm) is a 1.3-mile figure-eight trail off Maine Route 102A, with a cove and mudflats described by NPS.
- [Wonderland](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-wonderland-trail.htm) is a 1.4-mile round trip along an old gravel fire road to the rocky coast, also off Route 102A.

For tidepool timing, NPS says: “It is best to start tidepooling before low tide and to leave as the tide is rising.” Its full tidepooling and trail pages cover the local cautions; this guide makes no assurance that either shoreline is free of stranding or rising-water hazards.

## September–December: how much daylight is left?

This table covers each **whole month**, not just the days remaining after this update. A “daylight window” has at least 30 minutes of daylight within the below-+1.0-ft interval; a daylight-minus window also has a negative low.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| September | 38 | 21 | 10 | Fri Sep 11 · −0.48 ft · 5:40 PM · 58 |

| November | 39 | 14 | 8 | Sun Nov 22 · −0.27 ft · 2:27 PM · 55 |
| December | 43 | 16 | 8 | Wed Dec 23 · −1.38 ft · 3:48 PM · 58 |

*Computed September 9 from the Bar Harbor fact sheet. All four monthly best windows are in the Fair band. The fact sheet's 2026 records cover July 1–December 31, not the full year.*

The deepest daylight-overlapping low in that July–December record is December 24: −1.65 ft at 4:41 PM, with only 68 daylight minutes in the interval. December 23 is shallower at −1.38 ft but has 117 daylight minutes and scores 58 rather than 55. A window can overlap daylight even when its lowest point is after sunset. For the related high-tide season, see the [2026–2027 king-tides guide](/guides/king-tides-2026-2027-dates/).

For comparison, August's best window **was** Saturday August 15: −0.80 ft at 7:16 AM, 175 daylight minutes, score 75, Great. That date remains a useful summer reference, not a current recommendation.

## What has been recorded nearby?

The September 9 fact sheet's iNaturalist snapshot covers observations within 5 km of the reference station over the preceding 60 days. Its leading records are Common Periwinkle (19), Atlantic Dogwhelk (14), Common Atlantic Slippersnail (12), Atlantic Jackknife (6), and Soft-shelled Clam (4). These are observation counts near Bar Harbor, not a census of Ship Harbor or Wonderland and not a promise of what a visitor will find.

The [NPS tidepooling rules](https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/tidepooling.htm) say “collection is prohibited.” The same page gives the full marine-life and shoreline guidance.

Every tide figure above is a **prediction, not an observation**. The [methodology](/methodology/) explains the height threshold, daylight overlap, and score. Park access guidance and actual shoreline conditions are not outputs of that calculation.
