## Bar Island: the crossing rule is not our tide-window formula

The [National Park Service's Bar Island Trail page](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-bar-island-trail.htm), checked September 20, describes exposure “For 1.5 hours before and after low tide”. It warns that after missing the crossing, “it is nine hours until the bar emerges again to safely walk across.”

That official guidance and Tidewindow's below-+1.0-ft interval measure different things. Similar clock times do not establish that the gravel bar is exposed. The scores and intervals here do not confirm crossing conditions, reserve time on the island, or guarantee a return route. The linked NPS page contains the full warning and its local tide-chart link.

NPS lists the Bar Island hike at 1.9 miles round trip, including the gravel bar and island trail, starting from Bridge Street in downtown Bar Harbor. Its current access information also says downtown parking is paid from May 15 through October 31 and that parking is not allowed on the bar or Bridge Street. These details are separate from a low-tide prediction.

## Ship Harbor and Wonderland: different places, a regional reference

Tidewindow uses Bar Harbor as its reference station for all three listed Acadia destinations. That does not mean one exact tide time governs every shoreline: the [NPS tidepooling page](https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/tidepooling.htm) provides separate chart links for Bar Island and for Wonderland or Ship Harbor.

- [Ship Harbor](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-ship-harbor-trail.htm) is a 1.3-mile figure-eight trail off Maine Route 102A, with a cove and mudflats described by NPS.
- [Wonderland](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-wonderland-trail.htm) is a 1.4-mile round trip along an old gravel fire road to the rocky coast, also off Route 102A.

For tidepool timing, NPS says: “It is best to start tidepooling before low tide and to leave as the tide is rising.” Its full tidepooling and trail pages cover the local cautions; this guide makes no assurance that either shoreline is free of stranding or rising-water hazards.
