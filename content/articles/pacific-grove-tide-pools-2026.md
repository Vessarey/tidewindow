## Point Pinos, Asilomar, or Lovers Point?

The three areas share a tide station but differ in exposure and access:

- **Point Pinos / Pacific Grove Marine Gardens** — a broad rocky shoreline in the Pacific Grove Marine Gardens State Marine Conservation Area. CDFW says the area is popular for tidepools and that recreational take is limited to finfish; commercial hand harvest allowances apply only to giant and bull kelp. Tidepool visitors should leave marine life, shells, and rocks where they are.
- **Asilomar State Beach** — rocky pockets beside the Asilomar Coast Trail in the no-take Asilomar State Marine Reserve. California State Parks lists 27 trail entry points along Sunset Drive, adjacent roadside parking, no restrooms on the state beach itself, dogs on leash, and “No Fishing or Collecting.” CDFW notes that this side of the peninsula is exposed to open-ocean waves.
- **Lovers Point** — a more protected Monterey Bay-facing shoreline inside the no-take Lovers Point–Julia Platt State Marine Reserve. CDFW describes this side of the peninsula as sheltered from the ocean swell that reaches the outer coast.

Check the linked official pages for current posted restrictions before leaving. Tide predictions describe astronomical water levels, not waves, closures, or observed conditions.

## What are the no-take rules?

CDFW's rule for both Asilomar SMR and Lovers Point–Julia Platt SMR prohibits injuring, damaging, taking, or possessing living, geological, or cultural marine resources. Pacific Grove Marine Gardens SMCA uses the same baseline prohibition with narrow finfish and kelp exceptions. Those fishing and commercial-harvest exceptions do not turn shells, rocks, or tidepool animals into souvenirs.

California State Parks makes the visitor version especially plain at Asilomar: marine animals and features, including shells and rocks, are protected, and collecting is not allowed. Observe in place and use photographs as the record.

## How are these times computed?

NOAA Monterey station 9413450 publishes predicted highs, lows, and an hourly prediction series in feet MLLW. Tidewindow uses the hourly series to estimate when the water crosses the +1.0 ft planning threshold, rounds those window edges to five minutes, intersects them with computed sunrise and sunset, and scores the result. The formulas and limitations are published on the [methodology page](/methodology/).

The [Tide Window Finder](/tools/tide-window-finder/) accepts a ZIP code and selects the nearest of Tidewindow's 12 covered NOAA stations without sending or recording the ZIP. It compares modeled windows; it does not verify a beach route or an arrival time.
