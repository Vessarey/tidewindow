**As of November 13, 15 predicted minus lows at NOAA station 9410170 (San Diego) remain between Cabrillo's published 9:00 AM opening and 4:30 PM tidepool close through December 31, 2026, excluding holiday closures. The first is November 22 at 1:40 PM (−0.48 ft); the deepest is December 24 at 3:50 PM (−1.88 ft). These are time-of-low matches, not confirmed access or usable shore hours.**

Most tide pool guides give you the tide table and stop. At Cabrillo, the tide table is the easy half. The hard half is a gate.

## Does the tide table matter if the gate is closed?

Cabrillo National Monument sits at the tip of Point Loma. The [NPS hours page](https://www.nps.gov/cabr/planyourvisit/hours.htm), re-checked November 21, lists the main park at 9:00 AM–5:00 PM and the tidepools and coastal area closing at 4:30 PM. Thanksgiving and Christmas are closed days, and public access outside main-park hours is prohibited.

The comparison below uses those published hours, not a new arrival rule. It does not account for parking queues, temporary restrictions, walking time or conditions on the rocks. NPS also describes capacity-related road closures on its [tidepool page](https://www.nps.gov/cabr/learn/nature/tidepools.htm). A predicted low inside the clock interval is not a promise that a visit is possible.

## What does a tide window tell you about arrival?

It tells you when the predicted station water level is below a chosen threshold. Tidewindow's one-hour-before-low offset does not include travel, walking, queues, closures or a return margin, so this article no longer presents it as an arrival instruction. The [Tide Window Finder](/tools/tide-window-finder/) compares the next 30 days; its score and offset are not access advice. See the [methodology](/methodology/) for what is actually modeled.

NPS identifies 0.7 ft or lower as its tidepool-viewing benchmark. That is distinct from Tidewindow's +1.0 ft interval boundary. Predictions are not observations, and an interval is not a statement about surf or access.

## What are the rules once you're on the rocks?

The [NPS tidepool page](https://www.nps.gov/cabr/learn/nature/tidepools.htm), checked October 14, prohibits collecting natural objects and disturbing attached organisms, and directs visitors not to move rocks. Its footwear advice is explicit: "Shoes with good gripping soles are best". The linked page contains the full protection and visitor guidance.

The November 13 fact sheet's rolling 60-day iNaturalist observations within 5 km of the station include California sea hare, hooded nudibranch and lesser two-spot octopus. These are dated nearby observations, not a Cabrillo-only survey or a promise of what a visitor will see.

Standard [NPS entrance fees](https://www.nps.gov/cabr/planyourvisit/fees.htm), checked October 14, remain $20 per private vehicle, $15 per motorcycle and $10 per individual aged 16 or older entering on foot or bicycle. They are valid for seven days and include the tidepools. The fee page also lists group caps and pass exemptions.
