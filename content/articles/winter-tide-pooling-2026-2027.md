**Winter does not end West Coast tide pooling — it reschedules it. Across Tidewindow's 11 West Coast NOAA stations, December 2026 has 243 predicted lows below +1 ft MLLW; 113 of those windows overlap daylight by at least 30 minutes, 88 with minus-tide lows. Every one of the season's deepest daylight-qualifying lows lands in the afternoon, and the single highest-scoring upcoming window is Christmas Day at La Jolla (NOAA 9410230): −1.72 ft at 4:33 PM, score 90.**

The summer ritual on this coast is the dawn patrol: deep lows at 5–7 AM, covered in [our explainer on why summer's lowest tides happen at dawn](/guides/why-summer-lowest-tides-happen-at-dawn-pacific/). This page is that story's other half — what the same NOAA predictions say about November 2026 through March 2027, station by station. All numbers below come from fact sheets computed on November 6, 2026; they are predictions, not observations, and never a surf, weather, or access forecast.

## The seasonal flip: why winter lows move to the afternoon

West Coast tides are mixed semidiurnal — two unequal lows a day — and the sun's and moon's declination decides which of the two goes deep. NOAA's tide tutorial puts the sun at "its maximum declination, i.e., its largest angle to the equator" at the solstices, and the University of Washington's LiveOcean tide primer describes the regional consequence: the lower low water tends to happen in daytime in summer and at night in winter.

At night — mostly. The deep winter low drifts: part of the cycle still clips the end of daylight, which is why winter tide pooling survives as a late-afternoon activity. The computed evidence is lopsided in a way summer never is. In our October 1, 2026–March 31, 2027 window set, the five deepest daylight-qualifying lows at each of the 12 stations — 60 windows in all — **every single predicted low falls after noon**, between 1:45 PM and 7:06 PM local time. Not one morning among them. (Daylight-qualifying means the window overlaps sunrise-to-sunset daylight by at least 30 minutes; this ranking excludes nighttime-only lows, which can be deeper.)

Compare the July–December 2026 sample as a whole, where the busiest low-time hour across these stations is 6 AM — a bin fed almost entirely by the summer months. The dawn patrol clocks out; the sunset shift clocks in.

## Puget Sound takes the winter off

The flip is hardest on the inland Sound. In November 2026, Seattle (NOAA 9447130) has 21 predicted lows below +1 ft MLLW and **zero** windows with at least 30 minutes of daylight overlap; December repeats it (21 lows, zero daylight windows). Port Townsend (NOAA 9444900) is the same story: 22 lows in each month, zero daylight-qualifying windows. Every deep low at both stations falls in darkness.

It stays thin into the new year: all five of Seattle's deepest daylight-qualifying lows of the whole October–March season fall between February 15 and March 26, 2027, and the deepest of them is only −0.65 ft (March 25 at 1:45 PM). Port Townsend's five fall between January 18 and March 15, and its deepest — −1.64 ft on February 17 at 7:06 PM — has a window whose daylight portion ends around 5:37 PM, roughly 90 minutes before the low. If your beach is [Seattle](/beaches/wa/seattle-wa/) or [Port Townsend](/beaches/wa/port-townsend-wa/), winter is the season to study the [Puget Sound low-tide calendar](/guides/puget-sound-low-tide-calendar-2026/) and wait; the daylight lows come back with spring.

Counts are minus-tide windows with at least 30 minutes of daylight overlap; each month also has additional daylight windows whose lows sit between 0 and +1 ft. "Best" is the month's highest *score* — our 0–100 blend of depth, daylight, and timing per the [methodology](/methodology/) — not its deepest water. The two are routinely different in winter: Port Orford's deepest daylight-qualifying low of the entire season, −2.21 ft on December 24 at 6:14 PM, scores below its December 23 neighbor because its window's daylight runs out around 4:49 PM — the tide bottoms out in the dark.

Southern California is the honest winter destination. [La Jolla](/beaches/ca/la-jolla-ca/) carries 12 daylight minus-tide windows in November and 16 in December, with afternoon lows near 4–5 PM that sit comfortably inside daylight: Christmas Day's −1.72 ft low at 4:33 PM falls about 16 minutes before its window's daylight runs out (1:45 PM start plus 184 daylight minutes). That window scores 90 — the only Exceptional band in any station's next-60-day list.

## Three clusters to circle

The spring-tide rhythm concentrates winter's good windows into a few runs. In the current fact sheets, the per-station best windows pile up on:

- **November 23–26** — peaking Thanksgiving Day, November 26, at the Southern California stations (La Jolla: −1.40 ft at 4:46 PM).
- **December 22–25** — the deepest daylight-qualifying lows left in 2026 at the outer-coast and California stations, overlapping the [December king-tide dates](/guides/king-tides-2026-2027-dates/).
- **January 20–23, 2027** — appearing in nine of the twelve stations' seasonal top-five lists; San Diego's January 22 window (−1.96 ft at 3:30 PM) scores 90.

These clusters come from the stations' computed top lists, not an exhaustive calendar of every winter date — check your own dates in the [Tide Window Finder](/tools/tide-window-finder/) (nighttime lows can appear in its results), or let the [Trip Picker](/tools/trip-picker/) rank modeled low-tide windows across your chosen dates, up to 21 days at a time.

## The winter asterisk: surf, storms, and short light

A winter tide table is a weaker promise than a summer one. Pacific storm swell can erase a predicted low's usable reef entirely, and the daylight margin around a 4:30 PM low is thin — when the window says daylight ends at 4:49 PM, it means headlamps by 5. None of the numbers on this page say anything about surf, wind, rain, or access. Before any winter trip, read the National Weather Service surf-zone forecast for your stretch of coast, and read [what a sneaker wave is](/guides/what-is-a-sneaker-wave/) — winter is their season. If a storm has just passed, the reef may pay off in other ways: see [storm beachcombing on winter swell](/guides/storm-beachcombing-winter-swell/).

Predictions are not observations. Scores rank tide geometry, not safety or conditions, and a window with at least 30 minutes of daylight overlap does not promise daylight at the low — the arithmetic above shows how often those differ.
