**The Oregon coast's king tides for the 2026-27 season peak on December 23-25, 2026, with a second run on January 21-22, 2027. Per NOAA predictions the highest high is 10.58 ft MLLW at Newport (station 9435380) on Thursday, December 24 at 11:39 AM — and that same afternoon the coast drains to the season's deepest daylight low, -2.21 ft at Port Orford (station 9431647) at 6:14 PM.**

## What makes these tides "king"

Nothing official. NOAA describes "king tide" as "a popular, non-scientific term people often use to describe exceptionally high tides." The mechanism is a **perigean spring tide**: a new or full moon, when "the gravitational pull of the sun is 'added' to the gravitational pull of the moon on Earth," landing close to perigee, the moon's nearest approach. NOAA says the lineup happens "typically between 6-8 times each year." For the coast, the part that matters is that the swing works both ways. During any spring tide, per NOAA, "high tides are a little higher and low tides are a little lower than average," and perigee stretches both ends — the flood that tops a harbor seawall before lunch is the same event that empties the reef at Cape Arago before dark.

The full mechanism, and the same season at the other eleven stations we compute, is in [King Tides 2026 & 2027 Dates](/guides/king-tides-2026-2027-dates/). This page is the Oregon coast only, and it has both halves: the highs people photograph, and the lows worth a drive.

## Read the daylight column before the depth column

Every one of the twenty lows above arrives between 5:04 and 6:30 PM, which on the Oregon coast in late December means at or after sunset. The depth is real; the light is not. The number that decides whether a date is worth the drive is *daylight in window*: Port Orford's December 24 low is the deepest water of the season at -2.21 ft, but only 69 of its window's minutes are lit. Its January 21 low is a third of a foot shallower and has 152 lit minutes — more than double — and scores 76, the only Great-band window on this page.

That is the winter pattern everywhere on this coast: show up when the window *opens*, in mid-afternoon, and work the falling tide while you can still see it, rather than waiting for the low itself. Summer is the opposite — the [best Oregon tide pools guide](/guides/best-tide-pools-oregon-2026/) covers the dawn runs — and the [dawn-lows explainer](/guides/why-summer-lowest-tides-happen-at-dawn-pacific/) explains why the deep water migrates from morning to dusk as the year turns.

Daylight windows do not disappear as the season deepens; they thin out and shift. In October, November and December 2026 respectively, Port Orford gets 8, 10 and 11 daylight windows, Charleston 7, 8 and 10, Newport 7, 7 and 8, and Garibaldi 5, 5 and 6 — the south coast's advantage in depth is also an advantage in count.

Winter lows come with winter ocean. Predictions are not observations, and swell can erase a calm-water window entirely; what a sneaker wave is, and what the official agencies say about them, is in [What is a sneaker wave?](/guides/what-is-a-sneaker-wave/).
