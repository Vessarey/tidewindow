## What makes a tide “king”?

[NOAA describes king tide](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/kingtide.html) as an informal term for an exceptionally high tide. Spring tides occur around new and full moons, when solar and lunar effects combine to produce a larger tidal range. A perigean spring tide adds the moon's closer approach to Earth, but not every unusually high tide identifies a perigean event. Local tidal patterns and seasonal conditions also matter.

The [national 2026–27 king-tide guide](/guides/king-tides-2026-2027-dates/) compares all twelve stations Tidewindow covers. Neither a predicted high nor the size of the high-to-low swing guarantees flooding, exposed terrain or access.

## Daylight in the window is not daylight at the low

**Nineteen of these twenty lows occur after sunset.** The exception is Port Orford on January 21, 2027: its 5:12 PM low precedes the computed 5:16 PM sunset. Even there, the below-+1-ft window continues until 7:45 PM, so the whole window is not in daylight.

Port Orford's December 24 example shows the distinction especially clearly. Its -2.21-ft low occurs at 6:14 PM, after the computed 4:48 PM sunset. The window begins at 3:40 PM and contains 69 daylight minutes earlier in the afternoon. January 21 has 152 daylight minutes and the highest score among the twenty rows shown: 76, the selection's only Great-band result. That comparison says nothing about a particular beach being open or passable.
