**Puget Sound's daylight minus-tide season ends this week. After Thursday, September 10, Seattle (NOAA station 9447130) has no daylight minus tide left in 2026, and Port Townsend (9444900) keeps only one pre-sunrise sliver on October 5. The lows themselves don't stop — each station still logs 21–22 lows below +1 ft every month through December, just in darkness: Seattle's deepest tide of the season, −3.93 ft MLLW, arrives Christmas Eve at 10:58 PM. Full September tide charts for both stations are below.**

## How many of the deepest lows landed on a weekend?

Count the weekday column above: two Mondays, two Tuesdays, two Wednesdays, one Thursday, one Sunday. That's **1 weekend day out of 8, or 12.5%**. Run the same count on Port Townsend's four deepest daylight lows (July 14, 13, 15, 12) and you get the same lone Sunday: 1 of 4.

This isn't bad luck; it's arithmetic. Deep lows arrive in runs of four or five consecutive days around a new or full moon, and each day's low lands roughly 50 minutes later than the last. A run occupies whatever stretch of the week it occupies — and five consecutive days can touch at most two weekend days, usually fewer. In 2026 the big July run opened on a Sunday and spent its deepest three days on Monday through Wednesday.

## Seattle or Port Townsend?

Same moon, different clock. Port Townsend's lows run about 50 minutes ahead of Seattle's — 7:20 AM versus 8:12 AM on Labor Day — and its windows stretch longer once the sun is fully up: on Tuesday, September 8, Port Townsend got 251 minutes of daylight window against Seattle's 220. Early risers and photographers get more room to work up north.

Seattle's advantage is access. The station's reference beaches — Alki Beach, Constellation Park, and Golden Gardens — sit inside the city, and recent observations within 5 km of the station include 14 sightings of horned nudibranchs and 10 of white-lined dironas in the last 60 days (station data refreshed 2026-09-09). Full date lists and seven-day high/low tables live on the [Seattle station page](/beaches/wa/seattle-wa/) and the [Port Townsend station page](/beaches/wa/port-townsend-wa/); the [October calendar for Seattle](/beaches/wa/seattle-wa/2026-10/) and [for Port Townsend](/beaches/wa/port-townsend-wa/2026-10/) carry every high and low of the first darkness month, day by day.

## How do you work a window?

Use the arrive-by time, not the low time. For the next Exceptional window — Saturday, April 10, 2027, at Seattle — that's 1:02 PM, an hour before the 2:02 PM low. The National Park Service's tidepooling guidance says to be at the tidepools "at least an hour before low tide" so you can explore while the water is still receding, and to head back "no later than an hour after the tide has begun to rise." NPS also warns that the route out crosses slippery, algae-covered rock and pools a foot or more deep, so wear shoes that grip when wet. Taking children out on these flats? [Tidepooling With Kids](/guides/tidepooling-with-kids/) covers the checklist, the etiquette, and how to pick a window a small kid can finish.

The 2026 season ends quietly on a Thursday. The 2027 one opens on a Saturday in April with the best weekend Puget Sound has seen in two years. Set the calendar feed and forget about it until then.
