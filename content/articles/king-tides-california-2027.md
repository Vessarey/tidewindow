**California's official king-tide project dates are November 24–26 and December 23–25, 2026, then January 21–22, 2027, with additional dates at some northern locations. The clock varies along the coast: San Diego's November maximum is 7.45 ft MLLW at 8:34 AM on November 25 (NOAA 9410170); Monterey's is 6.86 ft at 9:39 AM (9413450). Below are local monthly peaks and a separate look at the low-water side.**

## California's official dates for the 2026–27 season

The [California King Tides Project](https://www.coastal.ca.gov/kingtides/), run by the California Coastal Commission, lists this statewide schedule, checked November 14, 2026:

| Project series | Statewide dates |
|---|---|
| November 2026 | November 24–26 |
| December 2026 | December 23–25 |
| January 2027 | January 21–22 |

The project also identifies **December 22, January 20 and/or January 23** for some northern locations. Its [local tide map and participation page](https://www.coastal.ca.gov/kingtides/participate.html) give location-specific details. Project dates provide a shared photography period; they do not mean every station reaches high water simultaneously.

[NOAA describes “king tide”](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/kingtide.html) as an informal name for an exceptionally high tide. The tables here are astronomical tide predictions, not observations or a forecast of storm effects.

## November high-tide times: San Diego to Half Moon Bay

All four covered California stations reach their **November maximum on Wednesday, November 25**. The southern pair peaks before 8:35 AM; Monterey and Pillar Point peak more than an hour later.

| Station and NOAA ID | Date (2026) | High (ft MLLW) | Pacific time |
|---|---|---|---|
| [San Diego](/beaches/ca/san-diego-ca/) — 9410170 | Nov 25 | 7.45 | 8:34 AM |
| [La Jolla / Scripps Pier](/beaches/ca/la-jolla-ca/) — 9410230 | Nov 25 | 6.96 | 8:29 AM |
| [Monterey](/beaches/ca/monterey-ca/) — 9413450 | Nov 25 | 6.86 | 9:39 AM |
| [Pillar Point Harbor / Half Moon Bay](/beaches/ca/pillar-point-ca/) — 9414131 | Nov 25 | 6.96 | 9:50 AM |

*Computed November 14 from every predicted November high in the fact sheets: 57 each at San Diego and La Jolla, 58 each at Monterey and Pillar Point. Values are rounded to two decimals; local times include the applicable Pacific time zone.*

The height reference matters. [MLLW means Mean Lower Low Water](https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/datum_options.html), a local tidal datum based on the lower low of each tidal day. A 7.45-ft tide at San Diego is a height above that station's reference zero. It is not 7.45 ft of water on a street, and the four local zeros are not a common land elevation.

## December and January: the monthly peaks

December's maximum lands on **December 24 at all four stations**. January splits: Monterey and Pillar Point peak on January 21, while San Diego and La Jolla peak on January 22.

| Station | Date | High (ft MLLW) | Pacific time |
|---|---|---|---|
| San Diego — 9410170 | Dec 24, 2026 | 7.56 | 8:29 AM |
| La Jolla — 9410230 | Dec 24, 2026 | 7.10 | 8:23 AM |
| Monterey — 9413450 | Dec 24, 2026 | 7.03 | 9:27 AM |
| Pillar Point — 9414131 | Dec 24, 2026 | 7.22 | 9:39 AM |
| San Diego — 9410170 | Jan 22, 2027 | 7.41 | 8:25 AM |
| La Jolla — 9410230 | Jan 22, 2027 | 6.95 | 8:19 AM |
| Monterey — 9413450 | Jan 21, 2027 | 6.82 | 8:30 AM |
| Pillar Point — 9414131 | Jan 21, 2027 | 7.11 | 8:43 AM |

*Computed November 14. Each station has 60 predicted highs in December and 60 in January. Every monthly maximum was checked against that complete month's record; January's values also appear in the fact sheets' October–March highest-five selection. These are monthly peaks at four stations, not annual records or a ranking of every California beach.*

For the full high/low sequence around a peak, open the station calendar. Two successive dates can have similar heights but different clock times. The [national king-tides guide](/guides/king-tides-2026-2027-dates/) provides the wider comparison without treating California's schedule as a national calendar.

## The low-water side: November 25 is different north and south

High-water timing does not tell you when the lower low occurs, or how much light remains. Here is the **lower of the two predicted lows on November 25** at each station, alongside the computed sunset and daylight overlap of its modeled below-+1.0-ft interval:

| Station | Lower low (ft MLLW) | Low time | Sunset | Daylight in window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| San Diego — 9410170 | −1.45 | 3:57 PM | 4:43 PM | 203 min |
| La Jolla — 9410230 | −1.51 | 3:55 PM | 4:43 PM | 213 min |
| Monterey — 9413450 | −1.53 | 5:18 PM | 4:53 PM | 143 min |
| Pillar Point — 9414131 | −1.77 | 5:20 PM | 4:53 PM | 144 min |

*Recomputed November 14 from NOAA lows and Tidewindow's station windows. All clock times are Pacific. Sunset is calculated for the station; daylight minutes measure the interval's overlap with sunrise–sunset, not time available on a beach.*

The San Diego and La Jolla lows occur before sunset. The Monterey and Pillar Point lows occur after it; their daylight minutes come earlier, while the predicted water is still falling. All four intervals clear Tidewindow's 30-minute daylight threshold, which illustrates why a “daylight window” does not necessarily contain a daylight low.

Neither the high-water peak nor the low-water interval applies park hours, route access or walking time. The [Cabrillo guide](/guides/cabrillo-tide-pools-2026/), [La Jolla guide](/guides/la-jolla-tide-pools-best-dates-2026/), [Pacific Grove guide](/guides/pacific-grove-tide-pools-2026/) and [Fitzgerald Marine Reserve guide](/guides/fitzgerald-marine-reserve-tide-pooling-2026/) keep those place-specific questions separate from the station predictions.
