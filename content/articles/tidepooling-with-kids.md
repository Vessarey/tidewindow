**Late-morning weekend lows are the whole game for tidepooling with kids, and 2026's best — Sunday, July 12's −3.2 ft low at 9:40 AM at Seattle, NOAA station 9447130 — has passed. What's left belongs to Southern California: across the 11 West Coast stations in our July–December fact sheets, only 9 of 66 best-of-month windows land on a weekend or holiday and score 80 or better, and all three still ahead are at La Jolla or San Diego.**

## What makes a tide window kid-friendly?

The adult version of tidepooling optimizes for one number: find the lowest low of the year, set an alarm. The kid version optimizes for everything else. Four things need to line up:

1. **A weekend or holiday.** Obvious, and the binding constraint — it eliminates most of the calendar before you look at a single height.
2. **A low that sits in daylight.** In summer that means mid-to-late morning, everyone fed and sunblocked and hours from naptime. In November and December it means checking the low against sunset, because several high-scoring windows put their low after dark.
3. **A long window.** Kids move at anemone speed. The July 12 Seattle window ran 295 minutes; families used all of them.
4. **A genuinely low low.** Below 0.0 ft MLLW at minimum; below −1 ft is where the good exposure starts.

The scoring on this site weighs depth, daylight, weekend-or-holiday timing, and season, which is why the ranking sometimes disagrees with the raw tide table. Seattle's deepest low of the season was −3.8 ft at 11:20 AM on Tuesday, July 14 — a spectacular tide that scored 90. Two days earlier, Sunday, July 12 was about six-tenths of a foot shallower at −3.2 ft, and scored 100. The Sunday won because it was a Sunday. That is exactly the trade a family should make, and the [trip picker](/tools/trip-picker/) makes it automatically; the [methodology page](/methodology/) shows the arithmetic. What the score does not model: naps, drive times, or whether a park is open that day. That part stays yours.

The counterexample is instructive. La Jolla's best-scoring July 2026 window was Wednesday the 15th: −1.5 ft at 4:54 AM. A 4:54 AM low means a predawn start — a birding trip, not a family outing.

**Puget Sound was the family jackpot.** The July 11–12 weekend scored 100 four separate times across the two stations: Saturday brought −2.3 ft at 8:49 AM to Seattle and −2.4 ft at 7:58 AM to Port Townsend, then Sunday went deeper still. If one kid melted down Saturday, Sunday was a full do-over. Seattle logged 20 daylight minus tides in July 2026 alone — minus lows whose window overlapped daylight by at least 30 minutes; the [Seattle station guide](/beaches/wa/seattle-wa/) lists every one. Sunday, August 9 offered an encore: −1.9 ft at Seattle (score 98) and −2.0 ft at Port Townsend (score 100). Of the 20 weekend-or-holiday windows scoring 80 or better in the whole July–December set, 13 are in Washington — and the last of them was Labor Day, September 7.

**Oregon's scores peaked early — literally.** All four of Oregon's 80-plus weekend windows landed on the same Sunday, July 12, with lows between 4:58 and 5:42 AM at Port Orford, Charleston, Newport, and Garibaldi. High scores, brutal wake-up: a reminder that the score measures the tide, not the toddler. The deep lows were midweek — [Newport](/beaches/or/newport-or/) bottomed out at −2.5 ft on both Tuesday, July 14 (7:04 AM) and Wednesday, July 15 (7:50 AM). The late-October Sunday at Newport was shallower and darker than its "weekend window" billing suggests: the −0.8 ft low at 6:37 PM came 22 minutes after the 6:15 PM sunset, and the window ran on to 8:30 PM in full dark.

Wherever you go, check the land manager's own page before promising a six-year-old anything.

## What goes in the bag?

Short list, learned the damp way:

- **Closed-toe shoes with grippy soles, for everyone.** The National Park Service at Cabrillo National Monument strongly discourages sandals and flip-flops; Olympic National Park suggests sturdy shoes you don't mind getting wet.
- **A full change of clothes per kid, left in the car.** Not because someone might sit in a tidepool.
- **The window, written down.** Date, low height and time, sunrise and sunset. Phone service is not a coastal guarantee.
- **Water, snacks, sun protection.** A five-hour window outlasts most snack supplies.
- **A trash bag.** Olympic National Park asks visitors to pack out any litter they find.
- **Nothing from Cabrillo's prohibited list:** no buckets or cups, no nets, no prying or probing tools. If it can scrape an animal off a rock, it stays home.

## What are the rules once you're on the rocks?

All of the following comes from the National Park Service — Cabrillo National Monument's tidepool guidance and Olympic National Park's tidepooling page (both linked in sources):

- **The eyeball rule.** Cabrillo's guideline is to touch animals "only as gently as you would touch your own eyeball." It is the single best sentence ever written for teaching a six-year-old restraint.
- **Nothing comes home.** Cabrillo prohibits collecting any natural item, "including living and dead organisms, shells or rocks." The empty shell counts.
- **No prying.** Olympic warns that forcing an animal off its spot can tear off its feet or squeeze its organs. If it resists, it stays.
- **Rocks go back exactly as found.** Peek under a rock, then return it to its original position and orientation.
- **Step on bare rock.** It's less slippery than algae-covered rock, and you avoid crushing the residents. Olympic's rule: do not leap from rock to rock; keep one foot on the ground.
- **Keep small children close.** Both parks say it plainly — Cabrillo notes that a kid's enthusiasm "can quickly translate into a slip or tumble."

## How do you time the day?

The arrival guidance worth having comes from the land managers, not the tide table: Olympic National Park's tidepooling page says to plan to reach the beach trailhead at least 30 minutes before the lowest tide. Follow the water out, and keep an eye seaward throughout — Olympic's page says to watch closely for the returning tide and "sneaker waves."

In November and December, add one habit: compare the low's clock time to sunset before you commit. Several of the season's remaining windows — Thanksgiving at La Jolla, Christmas at Monterey — put the low itself at or after dark, with the daylight earlier in the window.
